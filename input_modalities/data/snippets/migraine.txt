Migraine Assessment

A patient should be assessed for migraine using headache frequency, duration,
pain intensity, associated symptoms, aura type, and urgent warning signs.

Urgent medical assessment is required if any of the following are present:
sudden weakness, speech disturbance, loss of vision, loss of consciousness,
or the worst headache experienced so far.

If no urgent warning sign is present, record the number of headache days per
month and how many months the pattern has been present.

Chronic migraine is diagnosed when:
- headache occurs on at least 15 days per month,
- the pattern has lasted for at least 3 months,
- and at least two migraine-associated symptoms are present.

Episodic migraine is diagnosed when:
- headache occurs on 1 to 14 days per month,
- pain intensity is at least 4 on a scale from 0 to 10,
- and at least two migraine-associated symptoms are present.

Migraine-associated symptoms include nausea, vomiting, sensitivity to light,
sensitivity to sound, and worsening with physical activity.

Aura should be classified as none, visual, sensory, speech, or mixed.

Possible migraine is diagnosed when only one of the required symptom or
frequency criteria is missing.

If none of the above criteria apply, classify the result as
unlikely_migraine.
